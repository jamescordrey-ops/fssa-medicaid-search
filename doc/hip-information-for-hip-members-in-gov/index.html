Breadcrumbs FSSA Healthy Indiana Plan HIP Work Requirements Information for HIP Members Information for HIP Members Beginning January 1, 2027, Indiana Medicaid will implement work requirements for new applicants and existing Medicaid members eligible under the Healthy Indiana Plan (HIP). These requirements emphasize a balanced, values-driven approach: pairing compassion with member responsibility, ensuring that access to healthcare is supported by work, job training, education, and community participation. Certain HIP participants may be exempt. What are the Work Requirements Work requirements are activities HIP members and applicants must complete to be eligible.

Work requirements activities include: Employment Attending school at least half-time Participating in an apprenticeship Participating in a work program Volunteering in the community HIP members and applicants must spend at least 80 hours every month doing one of these activities or attending school or an apprenticeship at least half time. Members and applicants can combine activities to get to 80 hours during a month. Who must meet work requirements: Adults enrolled in HIP must meet the work requirements if all of these are true: Are ages 19-64 Are not pregnant Are not enrolled in or eligible for Medicare Work requirements exemptions: HIP members do not have to meet the work requirements for a month if, for at least one day that month, you: Are under 26 and were formerly in foster care. Are a member of a federally recognized Tribe.

Are a parent or caregiver of a child age 13 or younger. Are a caregiver of a person with a disability. Are a veteran with a total (100%) disability rating. Are pregnant, or it has been 12 months or less since your pregnancy ended.

Are in jail or prison or were released in the last 90 days. Are following the work rules for SNAP or TANF. Are in a drug or alcohol treatment program. Are medically frail or have special medical needs.

This includes: Being blind or having a disability. Having a substance use disorder. Having a serious mental health condition Having a serious physical, intellectual, or developmental disability. Having another serious or complex medical condition.

Other exemptions may also apply. You can meet the rule by working/volunteering/training for 80 hours, earning enough money, or being in school at least half time. Will medically frail be determined by the State or physician? Medically frail will be determined by the State. Physicians, and other clinicians, may provide recommendations on the medical history of their patients.

Does Substance Use Disorder treatment involve participation in drug court? SUD treatment may or may not include drug court for some individuals. However, drug court is not a factor in determining medically frail status, compliance with HIP Work Requirements or an exemption from the work requirements. Will there be any notification besides “non-compliance” if medically frail status is not met?

No, medically frail status is only one possible exemption and is not treated as a separate determination. If no activities or exemptions are present for a member, they will receive a Notice of Non-Compliance with HIP Work Requirements. Will the DFR provide a form for community service hours or just accept a statement from a community agency? A form will be available, and use of the form will ensure that all relevant information is provided; however, official documentation from the community agency (for example, a statement from a supervisor on their letterhead, with contact information included) would also be acceptable.

Does helping family or friends with tasks they can no longer do count as volunteer hours? This would not count as volunteer hours, but significant and consistent assistance for a child under age 14 or a disabled individual may qualify as family caregiving. A relative or household member providing such care may qualify for the family caregiver exemption. A non-relative who does not live with the individual for whom they provide care can have the caregiving hours counted towards the 80-hour requirement.

Does employment search conducted at a non‑profit count as an activity? No, this is not a countable activity. What if someone is off work for illness or surgery — how does this affect the $580 or 80 hours? Paid time off will be counted in the income calculation, but unpaid hours will not.

An individual requiring significant time off of work due to a medical issue may pursue a medically frail determination. Are there examples of how to convert income to hours? (Ex: gross 507.50 — how many hours toward the required 80?) To convert income into hours, divide the gross income by $7.25 (current federal minimum wage). For example, earnings of $507.50 in a month divided by 7.25 would equal 70 hours towards meeting HWR.

How does HIP Work Requirements affect Presumptive Eligibility? Presumptive eligibility will require pre-screening of compliance with HIP work requirements. More guidance will be released to providers on this requirement. Do work requirements apply to emergency services under Medicaid?

Individuals receiving limited benefits such as emergency services only or family planning only are not subject to the requirements. Is AI being used to process applications? No, artificial intelligence is not being used to process applications. How can boards or organizations assist members in completing the new forms?

One option is use of a Certified Navigators or Application Organization. These individuals are trained and available to assist members of the public with completing Indiana Health Coverage Program applications at no cost. More information is available here. If I have trouble finding opportunities to fulfill the 80-hour work/volunteer requirement, are there resources available?

Yes, there are several including Indiana 211, WorkOne Centers and Managed Care Organizations. Indiana 211: Indiana 211 is a free, confidential service connecting Hoosiers with local health and human services, including food, housing, utility help, health care, disaster recovery support and other community resources. Call 2-1-1, text your ZIP Code to 898-211 or visit IN211.org to find available resources in your community. WorkOne Centers: Need help finding a job, want to explore careers or get career advice?

Contact your local office to talk with an expert and learn about the programs and services available through WorkOne Centers. Managed Care Organizations (MCO): Individuals can contact their MCO and request assistance with locating resources that aid in meeting the work requirements. Anthem - 866-408-6131 CareSource Indiana - 844-607-2829 Managed Health Services - 877-647-4848 When do Work Requirements begin? Requirements begin for new applicants and redeterminations initiated on or after January 1, 2027.

For applicants in January, this means that requirements must be fulfilled for October, November, and December. Who must comply with HIP Work Requirements? Most HIP members ages 19–64 must meet work requirements unless they qualify for an exemption. What exemptions exist?

People who are pregnant, caring for kids, have disabilities or serious health needs, or drug or alcohol treatment programs, or meet other criteria don’t need to complete hours. What counts as medically frail? FSSA is still working with the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services on the medical frailty requirements for HIP Work Requirements exclusions, including the stipulation that the condition significantly impairs the individual’s ability to comply with work/community engagement requirements. More guidance will be made available.

Could members of my household have different redetermination dates? Yes, it is possible that members in the same household could have different redetermination dates. You can find your redetermination date in the Benefits Portal, under Assistance Group. Where can I find my redetermination date?

You can find your redetermination date in the Benefits Portal, under Assistance Group. Is it possible my redetermination date could change? Yes, and you will be notified if it changes. You can also find your redetermination date in the Benefits Portal, under Assistance Group.

Can you tell me when my three month lookback period will begin? For new applicants to be approved for HIP, the lookback period will be three months before the application month. Example: Apply in January, look back will be December, November and October. For members on HIP prior to 1/1/2027, the lookback period for determining compliance will be from their last redetermination.

Individuals will need to show compliance in any 3 months since their last redetermination. For example: March 2027 is redetermination datethe look back period will be April 2026 through March 2027. I received a Notice of HIP Work Requirements and was curious about the 80 hour requirement…does income over $580/month mean I don’t also need the 80 hours? Correct.

The requirement is either 80 hours of qualifying activity or income equal to federal minimum wage multiplied by 80 ($7.25 x 80 = $580). I am considering enrolling in an online college, would the classes have to be part of a degree program, and can they be online? Coursework at an accredited institution of higher learning, as well as high school or GED courses, can be counted. Classes may take place in-person or online.

I am self employed with a LLC partnership, so I do not have a paycheck, and the split income annually with my spouse. We both work 40–50 hours weekly. How should we maneuver this? An individual can provide documentation of the hours of self-employment and/or earnings by the most recent year’s tax returns or other business documentation.

I currently live off modest returns from personal investments. If I show $580/month or more, does that count as self employment without tracking hours? All income that is counted in Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) budgeting counts towards meeting work requirements. This would include any income which is taxable, as well as Social Security income.

What counts toward the 80 hours? Any mix of work, volunteering, classes, or job training counts. How is compliance checked? The state checks every three months.

If they can't confirm your hours or exemption, they’ll send you a notice asking for proof. What happens if someone does not comply? Coverage stops, and you must reapply. You also can’t get Marketplace financial help during this time.

How often are renewals? You renew HIP every six months, and the state checks your hours every three months. How do members report hours or changes? Report changes in work, school, health, or family within 10 days using the online portal, phone, mail, or a DFR office: Benefits Portal: Submit information to the Division of Family Resources via your FSSA Benefits Portal account at FSSABenefits.IN.gov Mail: FSSA Document Center, PO Box 1810, Marion, IN 46952 Phone: Call DFR at 1-800-403-0864 In-Person: Visit your local Office of the Division of Family Resources during regular business hours - 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., Monday – Friday, except for State Holidays What is the Benefits Portal?

The Benefits Portal is the easiest way to report hours and manage your HIP coverage. What should members do now? Make sure your info is correct, check if you have an exemption, plan how you’ll meet the rules, and set up an Benefits Portal account.
