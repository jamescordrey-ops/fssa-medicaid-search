Skip to main content Close Menu Information for HIP Members Beginning January 1, 2027, Indiana Medicaid will implement work requirements for new applicants and existing Medicaid members eligible under the Healthy Indiana Plan (HIP). These requirements emphasize a balanced, values-driven approach: pairing compassion with member responsibility, ensuring that access to healthcare is supported by work, job training, education, and community participation. Certain HIP participants may be exempt. What are the Work Requirements Work requirements are activities HIP members and applicants must complete to be eligible.

Work requirements activities include: Employment Attending school at least half-time Participating in an apprenticeship Participating in a work program Volunteering in the community HIP members and applicants must spend at least 80 hours every month doing one of these activities or attending school or an apprenticeship at least half time. Members and applicants can combine activities to get to 80 hours during a month. Who must meet work requirements: Adults enrolled in HIP must meet the work requirements if all of these are true: Are ages 19-64 Are not pregnant Are not enrolled in or eligible for Medicare Work requirements exemptions: HIP members do not have to meet the work requirements for a month if, for at least one day that month, you: Are under 26 and were formerly in foster care. Are a member of a federally recognized Tribe.

Are a parent or caregiver of a child age 13 or younger. Are a caregiver of a person with a disability. Are a veteran with a total (100%) disability rating. Are pregnant, or it has been 12 months or less since your pregnancy ended.

Are in jail or prison or were released in the last 90 days. Are following the work rules for SNAP or TANF. Are in a drug or alcohol treatment program. Are medically frail or have special medical needs.

This includes: Being blind or having a disability. Having a substance use disorder. Having a serious mental health condition Having a serious physical, intellectual, or developmental disability. Having another serious or complex medical condition.

Other exemptions may also apply. You can meet the rule by working/volunteering/training for 80 hours, earning enough money, or being in school at least half time. When do Work Requirements begin? Requirements begin for new applicants and redeterminations initiated on or after January 1, 2027. For applicants in January, this means that requirements must be fulfilled for October, November, and December.

Who must comply with HIP Work Requirements? Most HIP members ages 19–64 must meet work requirements unless they qualify for an exemption. What exemptions exist? People who are pregnant, caring for kids, have disabilities or serious health needs, or drug or alcohol treatment programs, or meet other criteria don’t need to complete hours.

What counts as medically frail? FSSA is still working with the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services on the medical frailty requirements for HIP Work Requirements exclusions, including the stipulation that the condition significantly impairs the individual’s ability to comply with work/community engagement requirements. More guidance will be made available. Could members of my household have different redetermination dates?

Yes, it is possible that members in the same household could have different redetermination dates. You can find your redetermination date in the Benefits Portal, under Assistance Group. Where can I find my redetermination date? You can find your redetermination date in the Benefits Portal, under Assistance Group.

Is it possible my redetermination date could change? Yes, and you will be notified if it changes. You can also find your redetermination date in the Benefits Portal, under Assistance Group. Can you tell me when my three month lookback period will begin?

For new applicants to be approved for HIP, the lookback period will be three months before the application month. Example: Apply in January, look back will be December, November and October. For existing HIP members, the lookback period will be three months before the renewal month or three months before the quarterly work requirements review. Example: Renewal month is May, look back will be April, March and February I received a Notice of HIP Work Requirements and was curious about the 80 hour requirement…does income over $580/month mean I don’t also need the 80 hours?

Correct. The requirement is either 80 hours of qualifying activity or income equal to federal minimum wage multiplied by 80 ($7.25 x 80 = $580). I am considering enrolling in an online college, would the classes have to be part of a degree program, and can they be online? Coursework at an accredited institution of higher learning, as well as high school or GED courses, can be counted.

Classes may take place in-person or online. I am self employed with a LLC partnership, so I do not have a paycheck, and the split income annually with my spouse. We both work 40–50 hours weekly. How should we maneuver this?

An individual can provide documentation of the hours of self-employment and/or earnings by the most recent year’s tax returns or other business documentation. I currently live off modest returns from personal investments. If I show $580/month or more, does that count as self employment without tracking hours? All income that is counted in Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) budgeting counts towards meeting work requirements.

This would include any income which is taxable, as well as Social Security income. What counts toward the 80 hours? Any mix of work, volunteering, classes, or job training counts. How is compliance checked?

The state checks every three months. If they can't confirm your hours or exemption, they’ll send you a notice asking for proof. What happens if someone does not comply? Coverage stops, and you must reapply.

You also can’t get Marketplace financial help during this time. How often are renewals? You renew HIP every six months, and the state checks your hours every three months. How do members report hours or changes?

Report changes in work, school, health, or family within 10 days using the online portal, phone, mail, or a DFR office: Benefits Portal: Submit information to the Division of Family Resources via your FSSA Benefits Portal account at FSSABenefits.IN.gov Mail: FSSA Document Center, PO Box 1810, Marion, IN 46952 Phone: Call DFR at 1-800-403-0864 In-Person: Visit your local Office of the Division of Family Resources during regular business hours - 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., Monday – Friday, except for State Holidays What is the Benefits Portal? The Benefits Portal is the easiest way to report hours and manage your HIP coverage. What should members do now? Make sure your info is correct, check if you have an exemption, plan how you’ll meet the rules, and set up an Benefits Portal account.
