Secondary regulation citation, if any: 5. State: Indiana 6. Region: Midwest Region 7. Regulatory requirements: The Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) regulations at 7 CFR 273 .1 0(f)(3) provide that the State agency must certify each eligible household for a definite period of time.

State agencies must assign the longest certification period possible based on the predictability of the household's circumstances. The first month of the certification period will be the first month for which the household is eligible to participate. The certification period cannot exceed 12 months except to accommodate a household's transitional benefit period. Federal rule 7 CFR 273.1 0(f)(l) provides that a State agency may ce11ify for up to 24 months households in which all adult members are elderly or disabled.

Additionally, 7 CFR 273.(f)(S) provides State agencies the ability to lengthen a household's current certification period once it is established, as long as the total months of the certification period do not exceed 24 months for households in which all adult members are elderly or disabled, or 12 months for other households. If the State agency extends a household's certification period, it must advise the household of the new certification ending date with a notice containing the same information as the notice of eligibility set forth in paragraph (g)(l)(i)(A) of this section. 8. Proposed alternative procedures: Indiana FSSA/Division of Family Resources (DFR) is requesting a waiver of the regulatory requirement and proposes to extend all certification periods by an additional twelve (12) months for recertifications that become due during the time Indiana is under a state of emergency due to COVID-19.

Additionally, the waiver would not require Indiana to send a notice to households regarding the extended certification period. Rather, Indiana would not send the notice of expiration until it would be due under the extended certification period. 9. In addition to the state-declared state of emergency, [Page 3] on March 13, 2020, President Trump declared a national state of emergency over the COVID­ 19 pandemic. Approval of this waiver will assist in protecting the lives; safety and health of the citizens of Indiana due to limiting personal contact (social distancing) to help prevent the rapid spread of this virus.

Regulatory requirements: The Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) regulations at 7 CFR 273 .2( e )(1) provide, in part, interviews may be conducted at the SNAP office or other mutually acceptable location, including a household's residence. Regulatory requirements at 7 CFR 273.2 (e)(2) provide, in part, the State agency may provide a home-based interview only if a household meets the hardship criteria and requests one. Also, the state agency must grant a face-to-face interview to any household that requests one.

Regulatory requirements at 7 CFR 273.2(e)(2)(i) provide that the State agency must inform each applicant of the opportunity for a face-to-face interview at the time of application and recertification and grant a face-to-face interview to any household that requests one at any time, even if the State agency has elected the option to routinely provide telephone interviews. Regulatory requirements at 7 CFR 273.14(b)(3) provide as part of the recertification process, the State agency must conduct an interview with a member of the household or its authorized representative at least once every 12 months for households certified for 12 months or less. The provisions of§ 273.2(e) also apply to interviews for rece1iification. The State agency may choose not to interview the household at interim rece1iifications within the 12-month period.

The requirement for an interview once every 12 months may be waived in accordance with § 273.2(e)(2). 8. Proposed alternative procedures: Indiana FSSA/Division of Family Resources is requesting a waiver of the regulatory requirement that requires the option for a face-to-face or in-home interview. Indiana will conduct all interviews by telephone as a result of the coronavirus (COVID-19) pandemic of 2019.

Indiana rules currently allow for telephone interviews at applications and recertification. 9.
