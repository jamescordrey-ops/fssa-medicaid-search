108-203, Sec. 431.A. DEFINITION 1. EITC The earned income tax credit (EITC) is a special tax credit that reduces the Federal tax liability of certain low income working taxpayers.

This tax credit sometimes results in a payment to the taxpayer, either as an advance from an employer or as a refund from IRS. 2. CTC The child tax credit (CTC) is a special refundable Federal tax credit that is available to certain low income taxpayers with earned income. They must be parents, step-parents, grandparents or foster parents with a dependent child.

This child tax credit may provide a refund to individuals even if they do not owe any tax. The CTC may result in a tax refund payment to the taxpayer from IRS beginning with tax year 2001. There is no advance payment with the CTC. B.

POLICY 1. The Exclusion a. 9-Month EITC ExclusionAny unspent Federal tax refund or payment made by an employer related to an EITC that is received on or after 3/2/04 is excluded from resources for the 9 calendar months following the month the refund or payment is received.b. Exception – 1-Month EITC Exclusion Any unspent Federal tax refund or payment made by an employer related to an EITC that is received before 3/2/04 is excluded from resources only for the month following the month refund or payment is received.c.

9-Month CTC Exclusion Any unspent Federal tax refund from a CTC that is received on or after 3/2/04 is excluded from resources for the 9 calendar months following the month the refund or payment is received. d. Exception – 1-Month CTC Exclusion Any unspent Federal tax refund from a CTC that is received before 3/2/04 is excluded from resources only for the month following the month the refund or payment is received.2. Interest on Unspent Payments Interest earned on unspent tax refunds related to an EITC or a CTC is not excluded from income and resources by this provision (SI 00830.500). C.

PROCEDURE – INITIAL CLAIMS AND POSTELIGIBILITY 1. When to Develop Receipt of EITC or CTC Develop the dates and amounts of EITC and/or CTC received only when an individual alleges the receipt of EITC and/or CTC and the resource exclusion is needed to permit eligibility for regular or conditional benefits, in that order. 2. Development and Documentation If an individual alleges that his or her resources include unspent EITC/CTC refunds or payments: Use the individual's Federal income tax return or other documents in the individual's possession to verify the source, date(s), and amount(s) of such refund(s) or payment(s); and Obtain a statement as to the date(s) and amount(s) of any account deposits corresponding to the EITC and/or CTC refunds or payments.

3. How to Determine the EITC and/or CTC Amount When the Tax Return Reflects a Refund But No Advance Payments Compare the tax credit to the amount overpaid/refunded. The EITC and/or CTC amount is the lesser of the two amounts. 4.

How to Determine the EITC Amount When an Individual Alleges or His/Her Tax Return Reflects Advance EITC Payments With or Without a Tax Refund Verify any advance payments using procedures in SI 00820.133 – SI 00820.145 (e.g., pay stubs, contact with employer, etc.). NOTE: Advance EITC payments (AEITC) are not wages and are identified separately on pay records. If a tax refund has been filed, verify AEITC per the prior bullet. The EITC amount is the AEITC payments plus the result of subtracting the AEITC payments from the tax credit on the Federal income tax return for the same period.

If the result of the second bullet (SI 01130.675B.4.) is zero, the EITC amount is the amount of the verified AEITC payments. If the result of the second bullet (SI 01130.675B.4.) is a positive figure (i.e., money is due the individual), the EITC amount is the verified AEITC payments received plus the lesser of that positive figure or the amount of tax overpaid. D. EXAMPLES 1.

No Advance EITC Payments Mr. Daniel undergoes a redetermination in May 2004. He alleges a $225 earned income tax credit, and receipt of a $114 tax refund in April 2004 which he deposited to his savings account. His tax return filed in 2004 for tax year 2003 shows the following: Advance EITC payment $0 Earned income tax credit $225 Amount overpaid/refund $114 The FO compares the tax credit ($225) with the amount overpaid/refund ($114) and determines that the lesser of the two amounts ($114) is the amount of the EITC payment.

In determining Mr. Donnell's resources, the $114 EITC would be excluded from resource counting beginning in May 2004 through January 2005. 2. EITC Credit Exceeds Advance Mrs.

Anne Finley undergoes a redetermination in April 2000. She received a tax refund of $690 in March 2000. She also received $600 in advance EITC payments in 1999 which she has not spent, and received $30/month advance EITC payments beginning January 2000. Her tax return for 1999 shows the following: Advance EITC payment $600 Earned income tax credit $1,150 Amount overpaid/refund $690 The FO verifies the amount and month of receipt of the advance EITC payments to date and subtracts the advance EITC payments for 1999 from the tax credit for the same period ($1,150 - $600 = $550).

The FO then compares that figure with the amount overpaid/refund and determines that the lesser figure ($550) is the amount of the EITC payment received in March 2000. If a resource determination is needed, the $550 would not be considered as a resource in April 2000. 3. CTC Payment Ms.

Powell undergoes a redetermination in May 2003. She alleges a $1800 child tax credit, and receipt of a $1450 tax refund in April 2002 which she deposited to her savings account. Her tax return filed in 2002 for tax year 2001 shows the following: Child tax credit $1800 Amount overpaid/refund $1450 The FO compares the tax credit ($1800) with the amount overpaid/refund ($1450) and determines that the lesser of the two amounts ($1450) is the amount of the CTC payment. In determining Ms.

Powell's resources, the $1450 CTC would be excluded from resource counting only in May 2002. Any remaining amount of the CTC would count as a resource beginning with June 2002. E.
