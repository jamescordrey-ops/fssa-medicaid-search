PROBLEM SOLVING COURTS IOCS Directory - PSC – Complete list of State of Indiana Problem Solving Courts Recovery Works – Specialty Court Registration Process 1. For Completion o The Problem-Solving Judge, administrator, or clerk completes the Recovery Works Specialty Court Form o Available at: www.in.gov/fssa/dmha/recovery-works/ → Navigate to Criminal Justice Partner – Specialty Court Form 2. Form Submission o Completed forms are sent to Recovery.Works@fssa.in.gov 3. Review and Follow-Up o Recovery Works reviews the submitted form and contacts the provider with any additional questions ▪ Providers are given 72 hours to respond 1.

How many hours per week do you attend this problem-solving court session (excludes individual staffing)? a) Only list actual attendance in court sessions b) Documentation must be kept for each session c) Providers must be actively involved in the court sessions each week 2. How many hours are staffed with the court each week? a) Staffing/Status updates alone do not suffice for the Problem-Solving Court exception b) Providers must be embedded in the actual PSC weekly process 3. Who is the point person for your agency for questions regarding PSC? a) Please provide a list of providers who participate in weekly problem-solving court sessions 4. The provider has read the Problem-Solving Court guidelines and understands the guidelines.

Yes? N? ▪ If no response is received, Recovery Works notifies the court that the form cannot be processed 4. Approval and Notification o Upon approval, Recovery Works sends information to the recovery liaison partners o The problem-solving court is cc’d on the communication confirming approval 5. Referrals o Referrals will automatically default to this court. o When a referral comes in, the liaison will send the referral to all providers registered to that court Eligibility for Problem-Solving Court Registration • • • • Eligible Providers o All Recovery Works Certified Treatment Providers are eligible to register with a problem-solving court.

Definition of Treatment Providers o Treatment providers are defined as agencies that: ▪ Hold a DMHA designation as ASO, ASR, CMHC, or ASAM Level 3.1/3.5 ▪ Providers must have a representative that actively participates in court sessions Exclusions o Recovery Residence Level II and III are excluded from eligibility, as they are not certified to provide treatment-related services. o Recovery Works funding may not be used for fulfilling criminal justice related requirements such as supervision, AOD screens etc. Dual-Service Agencies o Agencies that provide both treatment services and recovery residence will receive referrals for treatment services. o Participants may step down within the organization from an ASAM 3.1/3.5 service without requiring a new referral. Provider Guidelines – Problem-Solving Court Referrals • • • • Court Engagement o Providers must be actively engaged in the court, with weekly attendance alongside the individual referred. Required Documentation o Documentation must exist for each court session, including: ▪ Date ▪ Time ▪ Rendering Provider ▪ Session Outcomes ▪ Treatment Plan Goals Audit Requirements o Recovery Works will audit all problem-solving court provider referrals to ensure the above documentation is complete and accurate.

Referral Responsibility o Providers are responsible for ensuring that referrals received through problemsolving courts are directly related to treatment within the specific specialty court. Additional Information 1. Policy Adherence • • • Providers must fully comply with all Recovery Works Policies and Procedures. Non-compliance may result in corrective action, up to and including removal from the provider network.

Providers who are not actively attending the problem-solving sessions weekly are ineligible to bill for the participant. 2. Participant Eligibility Verification • • Eligibility documentation must be retained for audit purposes. Providers must ensure individual is involved in individual problem-solving court 3.

Billing Standards • • • • All billing must align with Recovery Works standards. Billing practices must maintain fidelity to the Recovery Works model and ensure transparency, accuracy, and compliance. PSC exception may not be used for weekly/monthly status updates. Providers must be imbedded in the PSC weekly court process with the participant.

Recovery Works will review all PSC attendance and documentation at the time of an audit 4. Proper Use of PSC Designation • • • PSC designation may not be used to circumvent the Recovery Works referral process. PSC designation is solely for providers who are imbedded in the PSC court process All Recovery Works providers are required to provide monthly updates to CJP’s upon request. 5.

Referral Pathways • • • Referrals for individuals not directly involved in a designated PSC must follow the standard Recovery Works referral process. The PSC referral pathway cannot be used as a substitute for probation, community corrections, work release, or other referral processes. All referral pathways must be documented and auditable.
