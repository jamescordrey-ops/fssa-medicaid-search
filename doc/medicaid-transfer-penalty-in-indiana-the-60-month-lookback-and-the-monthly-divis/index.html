Medicaid transfer penalty in Indiana: the 60-month lookback and the monthly divisor

Reference notes Indiana

Program: General

Source: Find on FSSA site ↗

If a person gives away money or property, or sells it for less than it is worth, and then applies for Medicaid to pay for long-term care (a nursing home, or waiver services), Indiana can impose a transfer penalty: a period of months when Medicaid will not pay for the long-term care. The lookback period is 60 months. When a person applies, Indiana reviews transfers made during the 60 months before the application. A gift to a child, giving money toward a relative's house, deeding property over, or donating assets can all count as transfers if the person did not receive fair value in return.

The length of the penalty is the total value given away divided by a monthly divisor that Indiana sets from the average monthly cost of private nursing facility care. The divisor depends on the application date: - For applications filed on or after July 1, 2026, the divisor is $8,027 per month. - For applications filed from July 1, 2025 through June 30, 2026, the divisor was $7,651 per month. Example: a gift of $40,135 with an application filed after July 1, 2026 gives $40,135 divided by $8,027, which is 5.0 months of penalty. During those months the person is not eligible for Medicaid payment of long-term care services, even if they otherwise qualify.

The divisor and the transfer rules are in the Indiana Health Coverage Programs Policy Manual, section 3006.00.00 (average monthly cost of private nursing facility care) and section 2640.10.35 (transfers of property). Giving assets away does not pause or avoid the penalty: the transfer is counted when the person applies within the 60-month lookback.
