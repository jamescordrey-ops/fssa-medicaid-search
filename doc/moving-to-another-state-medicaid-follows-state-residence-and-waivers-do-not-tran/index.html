Moving to another state: Medicaid follows state residence and waivers do not transfer

Reference notes Indiana

Program: General

Source: Find on FSSA site ↗

Medicaid is run state by state, and eligibility follows state residence. Under the federal rule (42 CFR 435.403), a state Medicaid agency provides Medicaid to eligible residents of that state. For a person age 21 or over who is not in an institution, the state of residence is the state where the person is living and intends to reside, or entered with a job commitment or seeking employment. A state may not deny Medicaid eligibility because the person has not lived in the state for a specified period: there is no minimum time-in-state requirement.

Because of that structure, Medicaid and Medicaid waiver services do not transfer when a person moves. An Indiana HCBS waiver (such as the Family Supports Waiver, the CIH Waiver, or the Health & Wellness Waiver) is an Indiana Medicaid program; it does not pay for services in another state, and another state's waiver does not pay in Indiana. A family moving into Indiana starts over with an Indiana application, and a family leaving Indiana applies in the new state and follows that state's process, including that state's developmental-disability agency and any waitlist there. Practical points: apply in the new state as soon as the move is made; expect a possible gap in services while the new state processes the application and any waiver enrollment; and keep records from the old state (assessments, plans of care) to give the new state's case managers.
