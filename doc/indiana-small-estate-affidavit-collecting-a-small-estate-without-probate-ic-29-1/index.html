Indiana small estate affidavit: collecting a small estate without probate (IC 29-1-8-1)

Reference notes Indiana

Program: General

Source: Find on FSSA site ↗

Indiana lets the people entitled to a small estate collect the property without opening a probate case, using a sworn statement called a small estate affidavit. The rule is Indiana Code 29-1-8-1. The estate qualifies when the value of the gross probate estate, wherever located, minus liens, encumbrances, and reasonable funeral expenses, does not exceed $100,000. That $100,000 limit applies to a person who died after June 30, 2022.

(For deaths from July 1, 2006 through June 30, 2022 the limit was $50,000.) There is a waiting period: the affidavit can only be used after 45 days have passed since the death. It also cannot be used if an application or petition to appoint a personal representative is pending or has already been granted in any court. The affidavit is made by or for a distributee (a person entitled to a share) and must state: (1) the estate value is under the limit; (2) 45 days have elapsed since the death; (3) no personal representative application is pending or granted; (4) the name and address of each distributee and the part of the property each is entitled to; (5) that the affiant has notified each distributee of the intention to present the affidavit; and (6) that the affiant is entitled to payment or delivery of the property on behalf of each distributee. When the affidavit is presented, whoever owes the person who died money, or holds their personal property (a bank account, a stock, or another instrument), must pay or deliver it to the distributee.

The same route works for a life insurance death benefit owed to the estate, for property in the person's safe deposit box, and for the person's digital assets. A transfer agent must change the registered ownership of a security on the corporation's books when presented with the affidavit. A motor vehicle or watercraft title can be transferred sooner: if 5 days have elapsed since the death and no personal representative appointment is contemplated, the Bureau of Motor Vehicles transfers the title on an affidavit stating the estate-value condition and the affiant's entitlement. The affidavit route covers personal property and payments.

It is not a way to transfer title to real estate.
