MEMO: United States Department of Agriculture, Food and Nutrition Service, 1320 Braddock Place, Alexandria, VA 22314. DATE: August 21, 2026.

SUBJECT: SNAP - Fiscal Year 2027 Cost-of-Living Adjustments. TO: All State Agencies, Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program. USDA annually adjusts SNAP maximum allotments, income eligibility standards, and deductions. These COLAs are effective October 1, 2026.

Attached are the FY 2027 COLAs for the 48 contiguous States and the District of Columbia, Alaska, Guam, Hawaii, and the U.S. Virgin Islands. Maximum allotments increase for the 48 States and D.C., Alaska, Guam, and the U.S. Virgin Islands.

The maximum allotment for a family of four in the 48 States and D.C. is $1,023. In Alaska it ranges from $1,306 to $2,027. In Guam it is $1,507 and in the U.S. Virgin Islands $1,315.

In Hawaii it decreases to $1,655. The minimum benefit for the 48 States and D.C. increases to $25. Per Section 3(u)(2) of the Food and Nutrition Act, households of nine or more persons receive 22 percent of the maximum four-person allotment per person; total benefits for additional persons are capped at 200 percent of the maximum four-person allotment, so the maximum allotment for households with 18 or more persons is capped at $3,887 in the 48 States and D.C. The shelter cap increases to $769 for the 48 States and D.C., and also increases for Alaska, Guam, Hawaii, and the U.S.

Virgin Islands. The maximum homeless shelter deduction increases to $205.66 for the 48 States and D.C., Alaska, Guam, Hawaii, and the U.S. Virgin Islands. The standard deduction for household sizes one through three increases to $217 per month for the 48 States and D.C.

The asset limit remains $3,000; for households with at least one member age 60 or older or disabled it increases to $4,750. Signed: Sasha Gersten-Paal, for Ronald Ward, Acting Associate Administrator, Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program. Digitally signed 2026-08-21.
