SNAP National Accuracy Clearinghouse (NAC) Resource Type Training and Outreach Related Content Interim Final Rule: SNAP: Requirement for Interstate Data Matching to Prevent Duplicate Issuances Implementation of the National Accuracy Clearinghouse Interim Final Rule Memo Q&As Concerning SNAP - NAC Interim Final Rule Second Q&As Concerning SNAP - NAC Interim Final Rule Third Q&As Concerning SNAP - NAC Interim Final Rule The SNAP National Accuracy Clearinghouse (NAC) is an innovative technology solution designed to prevent Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) participants from receiving benefits in multiple states. All SNAP state agencies are working toward nationwide implementation of the NAC, an interstate data matching system. The NAC's primary goal is to enhance program integrity, reduce improper payments, increase customer experience and ensure fair and accurate distribution of SNAP benefits to eligible recipients across state lines. Commitment to Security and Program Integrity FNA is fully committed to the successful nationwide implementation of the NAC.

FNA has ensured that the NAC uses advanced security measures to protect the personally identifiable information of SNAP participants. FNA also has built in safeguards to ensure households receive benefits for which they are eligible and are not incorrectly removed from the program. Skip Map NAC State Implementation The NAC went live on Feb. 5, 2024, when Iowa and Montana became the first two states to launch the NAC matching program.

We have commitments from all SNAP state agencies to implement the NAC ahead of the October 2027 regulatory deadline. We support each state agency throughout its NAC implementation project, which includes developing connections from the state eligibility system to the NAC system and updating internal business processes to allow state workers to take timely action to prevent duplicate participation as they process SNAP cases. In response, FNA passed an interim final rule, requiring state agencies to participate in the NAC and take appropriate action based on indications from the NAC that an individual may already be receiving SNAP benefits from another state agency.
