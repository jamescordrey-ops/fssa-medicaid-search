Forms Included in this Packet: 1.) Appearance • This is the form that tells the court how to contact you if they need to. 2.) Veriﬁed Motion for Continuance • This is the form that tells the court you can’t attend court on the date of your hearing and asks the court to move your hearing to another date and time. 3.) Order on Veriﬁed Motion for Continuance • This is the form that the judge must sign to grant your continuance request. If granted, the judge will schedule your hearing for a new date and time.

You should show up to court on the date and time written on this order. Page 2 of 3 Last Updated September 2024 www.indianalegalhelp.org Terms You May Need to Know Continuance- A "Continuance" is when a person asks the court to delay a trial or hearing. You can do this if you have a good reason, which you need to show by giving proof, like a written statement. These forms will give you the place to provide the written statement.

The court will decide if it’s okay to postpone the hearing. Related Cases- Sometimes there can be other cases that have the same parties. Here are some examples of “related cases: In family law cases, like divorce or paternity, if the Department of Child Services is involved with the family. Also, it’s possible that someone other than a parent(s) was responsible for caring for the child(ren), like in a Minor Guardianship case.

In an eviction case there might be a separate case that is about any damages owed. In a probate/estate case, if there used to be an adult guardianship case related to the person that died. Perjury- When a person makes a false statement under oath in the things they say in case documents or when they testify in court, when they know the statement is false or they don’t believe the statement is true. Certificate of Service- Note*: You will need to know these options each time you have to tell the court how you served court papers to the other person in the case.

Come back to this definition when you need to remember what your options are, like when you are completing the Certificate of Service sections in the forms included in this packet. When a court case begins, it's important for both sides to know about it and receive official forms. This is called "service of process." These forms tell them about the case and their rights. You are required to give a copy of every form you file with the court to every other person involved in the case.

You must say when you gave them the copies and how you gave them the copies. Here are the ways to get them their copies: First Class U.S. mail- You can serve the court forms by paying to have them sent by certified mail. The clerk will ask you to complete the Certified Mail card. The clerk will serve the court forms by Certified Mail.

Certified mail ensures that the court forms have been signed for and received. Hand Delivery- You can serve the court forms by paying to have a sheriff or private company handdeliver them to the other party. Ask the clerk for information on how to use the Sheriff’s office to serve your court forms. Indiana’s E-Filing System- You can serve the court forms by filing your documents electronically.

Ask the clerk for information on how to e-file your court forms.
