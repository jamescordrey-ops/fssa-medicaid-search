If so, you should know about special Supplemental Security Income (SSI) rules. These rules can help you keep Medicaid and may help you keep getting some SSI even though you are working. Different rules apply to Social Security Disability benefits.

How Work May Change Your SSI If you work full-time or part-time and make $65 or less each month, your SSI will usually not change. If you make more than $65 each month, we only count half of your earnings above $65 when we figure the amount of your SSI. If you are a student under age 22, we may exclude a higher amount of your earnings. The amount of earnings we do not count is adjusted every year based on the cost of living.

Medicaid If you get Medicaid, it will usually continue as long as you get SSI. Often you can keep your Medicaid even when you start earning too much money to get SSI as long as you are blind or disabled. For example, you can keep your Medicaid if you can't afford to replace it or to pay for attendant care services you get now. We Don't Count Some Of Your Expenses If you are blind or disabled, the earnings you use for some of your working expenses will not count as income.

For example, we don't count the cost of special equipment that helps you to work. A Plan Can Help If you are blind or disabled, you may be able to keep more of your SSI if you develop a special plan to support yourself. We call this a plan for achieving self-support (PASS). This plan lets you set aside money for a certain amount of time for a work goal.

For example, you may set aside money to start a business, go to school, or get training for a job. We don't count what you set aside when we figure your SSI. This can help keep you on SSI or help you get more SSI. A PASS may also help someone you know qualify for SSI.

If You Need Help Finding a Job If you are blind or disabled, you can ask someone at your State vocational rehabilitation office to help you find a job or give you training. If You Have Any Questions If you want to know more about these rules contact any Social Security office and ask to speak to someone about work incentives.
