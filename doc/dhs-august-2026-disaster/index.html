DHS: August 2026 Disaster Skip to main content Close Menu August 2026 Disaster August 2026 Disaster Information Hub What Happened During the August 2026 Disaster? On the morning and afternoon of Aug. 11, 2026, a thunderstorm system, preliminarily classified by the Storm Prediction Center as a derecho, crossed from Illinois into northwest and north-central Indiana. The system produced extreme straight-line winds, at least two tornadoes, large hail and flash flooding.

As the system tracked south and east through the late morning and afternoon, additional damage was reported across central Indiana. This included a large swath of power outages and numerous reports of tree damage. This severe weather event also included repeated rounds of intense precipitation, which shifted concern to excessive flooding early on Aug. 12.

Reports of 6-9 inches of rain were seen in some areas of that state, with additional rain in the days following. Multiple rivers reached their major flood stage and numerous reports of road closures and localized evacuations due to flooding occurred. This page will serve as an information hub related to this event and will continually be updated with new information and resources when they become available. Frequently sought after information as well as helpful resources can be found below.

Substantial Damage Property Assistance Program The IDHS Substantial Damage Property Assistance Program helps local communities and property owners navigate substantial damage requirements following 2026 severe storms and flooding. The program provides technical assistance to FEMA Public Assistance-declared counties and their local municipalities to support the evaluation and documentation of affected residential and commercial structures located in regulated floodplains.

After a disaster, local communities are required to assess damaged structures in mapped flood hazard areas (floodplains) to determine whether they meet the definition of substantial damage. Generally, a structure is considered substantially damaged when the cost to restore it to its pre-damage condition equals or exceeds FEMA’s minimum standard of 50% of the structure’s pre-damage market value. Local ordinances may establish different or additional requirements. A substantial damage determination may affect the requirements a property owner must meet when repairing or rebuilding a damaged structure.

Local governments are responsible for making all official substantial damage determinations in accordance with their local floodplain ordinances. Assistance for Property Owners Property owners with questions about the substantial damage assessment process can contact the Substantial Damage Property Assistance Line at 833-495-9888. The assistance line is available Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. ET.

Call center assistance staff can: Explain the substantial damage assessment process Answer general questions about permitting and rebuilding requirements Help property owners understand potential next steps Connect callers with appropriate local officials or recovery resources when additional assistance is needed The assistance line provides general information and guidance. It does not determine whether a property is substantially damaged or replace the authority of local officials. This hotline is not available for questions related to the State Disaster Relief Fund. For general questions about substantial damage assessments, permitting, rebuilding or next steps, call the Substantial Damage Property Assistance Line at 833-495-9888, Monday through Friday from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.

ET. Interested parties can also view the Substantial Damage Property Assistance Program FAQs for more information. SNAP Replacement Benefits On September 17, 2026, the Indiana Family and Social Services Administration (FSSA) received federal approval for an Automated Mass Replacement waiver through the U.S. Department of Agriculture’s Food and Nutrition Administration.

This federally authorized waiver allows Indiana to automatically replace a portion of monthly SNAP benefits for recipients living in designated ZIP codes within affected counties of the Aug. 2026 severe weather event. The replacements are approved based on the date SNAP recipients’ allotments were originally received prior to the storm. For more information, please view the Governor's SNAP replacement press release.

Indiana Housing and Community Development Authority's Temporary Housing Grants The Indiana Housing and Community Development Authority (IHCDA) is providing temporary housing grants to help eligible displaced Hoosiers transition from emergency shelters into hotels, apartments and other temporary accommodations. Hoosiers in need of temporary housing can find more information at IN211.communityos.org or by calling 211.

Survivor's with a home that was impacted by the August disaster and who are still in need of housing assistance should contact Indiana 211 by Monday, Oct. 19 to apply for IHCDA's hotel voucher. The last day to apply for IHCDA's hotel voucher is Monday, Oct. 19.

IHCDA’s disaster assistance offers two options to support households displaced by recent flooding and storms in Indiana. An eligible household must select only one of the two options. Please be aware that IHCDA will reach out via email to follow up on temporary housing vouchers. Residents seeking assistance should check their spam folders if they do not receive a follow-up email in their main inbox.

If no follow-up email is received, please contact 211 by calling 211 or 866-211-9966. Vouchers for Temporary Hotel StayApproved households are eligible for a hotel stay for up to 60 days (extended from 30 days). If a household is able to return home sooner, the hotel assistance will end at that time. Residents currently taking advantage of the voucher program are approved for the extension allowing for an additional 30 days from their current voucher start date.

Rental AssistanceApproved households who know they cannot return to their home may choose rental assistance to move to a new rental unit. In those cases, IHCDA covers the application fee, one month’s rent (not to exceed the HUD published Fair Market Rent based on the county and unit size) and the security deposit (not to exceed the lesser of the deposit amount in the lease or two times the Fair Market Rent for the unit). If the actual cost of rent is higher, the household is responsible for the difference. Households do not receive direct cash benefits from IHCDA.

All payments are made directly to hotels or landlords, and total costs vary based on the hotel or apartment chosen, unit size and local market conditions. Survivor's still in need of temporary housing assistance beyond the voucher program should apply for FEMA's Individual Assistance by visiting DisasterAssistance.gov or by calling 1-800-621-3362.
