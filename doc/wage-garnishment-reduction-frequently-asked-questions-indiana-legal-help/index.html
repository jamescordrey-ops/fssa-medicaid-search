Under Indiana law, the most that can be taken from your paycheck is the smaller of these two amounts: 25% of your disposable earnings (your pay after required taxes are taken out), or Any amount of your disposable earnings over $217.50 per week Your employer calculates both amounts and takes whichever is smaller. This is called the “standard” or “maximum” garnishment amount. Can I Ask The Court To Reduce My Wage Garnishment?

Yes. Indiana law allows you to ask the court to reduce your wage garnishment if the current amount is causing you financial hardship. You file a Motion to Reduce Wage Garnishment with the court that issued the original garnishment order. You must show the court that the current garnishment is making it very difficult to pay for basic living expenses like housing, food, utilities, transportation, and medical care.

What Might Count As "Financial Hardship"? Financial hardship means the current garnishment amount is making it very difficult for you to pay for basic needs for you and your household. Some examples might include: Recently returning to work after being laid off, or reduced work hours or pay Increased expenses for rent, utilities, daycare, or medical care Facing eviction, foreclosure, or utility shut-off Someone in your household lost their job or became ill Unpredictable or seasonal work hours Having to miss work often due to health problems or transportation issues Recent additions to your household, like a new baby What Is The Lowest My Garnishment Can Be Reduced To? Under Indiana law, garnishments can be reduced down to 10% of your disposable earnings.

If you earn less than $242 per week in disposable earnings, the minimum might be even lower than 10%. By law, the garnishment cannot be reduced below these minimum amounts. What Kinds Of Income Are Protected From Garnishment? Certain types of income cannot be garnished to pay most debts, including: Social Security benefits Supplemental Security Income (SSI) Veterans (VA) benefits Most other federal benefits These benefits are protected under federal law, even after you receive them.

If you keep this protected income in a separate bank account from your other money, it will be easier to prove the money is exempt if someone is trying to garnish your account.
