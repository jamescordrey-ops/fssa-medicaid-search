Skip to main content Close Menu Grant Opportunities School Age Child Care GrantThe Office of Early Childhood and Out of School Learning will support licensed/regulated school-age child care programs in public school corporations, as authorized by IC 12-17-12, or a child care program that is also a not-for-profit organization, exempt from federal income taxation under Section 501(c) (3) of the Internal Revenue Code. Applicants may request a maximum of $40,000 per county. As outlined in IC 12-17-12-16, awarded Grants cannot exceed 90% of the Grantee’s total annual program operating costs. This is a cost reimbursement grant; thus, no funds will be disbursed in advance.

Programs must have been open and operating their school-age child care program for at least one (1) full year preceding the date of application for the School Age Child Care grant and remain in good standing through the entire grant period, and serve children between the ages of five (5) and fifteen (15) years which includes:Before and/or after school care offered during the regular school year or during the summer, if summer school is offered.Care provided during periods when school is not in session, excluding summer breakBefore and/or after school care for children who attend kindergarten (enrolled in either a full-day or half-day program).The SACC grant will be released on January 14, 2026, and will close on February 13, 2026. The School-Age Child Care Project Fund supports a school age child care program which offers care to children between the ages of five (5) and fifteen (15) years and includes:Before and/or after school care offered during the regular school year or during the summer, if summer school is offered.Care provided during periods when school is not in session, excluding summer breakBefore and/or after school care for children who attend kindergarten (enrolled in either a full-day or half-day program). What is the high-level timeline for the SACC grant?

The grant will be released on January 14, 2026, and will close on February 13, 2026. Applications will be reviewed for mandatory requirements before evaluations begin. Once evaluations are complete, the applications will be scored and designated for award. Once awards are approved by the state, grantees will be notified of their award status.

This timeline can take anywhere from two (2)-four (4) months depending on the quantity and quality of applications. Following award, grantees will work with the state to sign their grant agreements for contract routing and the grant term will begin on July 1, 2026. Eligibility FAQs What programs are eligible to receive the SACC grant funding?

Eligibility guidelines are as follows:The SACC grant is open to programs that are licensed or regulated by the Office of Early Childhood and Out-of-School Learning (OECOSL) and offer care to children between the ages of five (5) and fifteen (15) years and offer care as follows:Before and/or after school care offered during the regular school year or during the summer, if summer school is offered.Care provided during periods when school is not in session, excluding summer breakBefore and/or after school care for children who attend kindergarten (enrolled in either a full-day or half-day program).To be eligible to receive funding under this grant, the before and after school care program, not just the pre-school program must: Be active, licensed or regulated by OECOSL, and compliant with any required IDOE regulations.Comply with all applicable federal, state, and local licensing laws, statutes, and regulations.Comply with Indiana statutes and regulations that govern child care facilities and be in compliance with the State Fire Marshall requirements.Not be on a probationary license or certificate of non-compliance at the time of application.Maintain status as a licensed, regulated, or registered child care provider as defined by IC 12-7-2-15, IC 12-17-12-5, IC 12-17.2-6, IC 12-17.2-2-8, and IC 12-7-2-28.2 through 12-7-2-29 (until closure and verified by OECOSL). OECOSL may perform a site visit to any program applying to aid in the evaluation response.Meet and maintain CCDF Provider Eligibility Standards (PES), even if the program does not currently have any voucher children.Be in good standing with the Secretary of the State, the Indiana Department of Revenue and Indiana Workforce Development.Remain in good standing with OECOSL from the time of application through the end of the grant period.Have provided child care services before and/or after school to school-age children for at least one (1) full school year before the date of application for this grant.Note: A program that has a 501(c)(3) status isn't automatically eligible unless it is also licensed or regulated by OECOSL. This would mean that someone from OECOSL makes routine visits to the program site as part of the program’s licensing/regulation agreement with the state. What does it mean to be a program in “good standing”?

The term "in good standing" for purposes of the grant agreement is defined as follows: the grantee and its principals are both in compliance with all required CCDF repayments, any past/present/future grant agreement terms or required repayments from any other grants awarded to the program and have no current unresolved licensing fines or licensing violations. Grant Details' FAQs When is the deadline to apply? The SACC Application opens Jan 14, 2026 and will close on February 13, 2026. All applications must be submitted by close of business on February 13, 2026.

How can SACC grant funds be allocated? Funds can be allocated across any or all of the four following budget categories:Program Enrichment - This may include the cost of developmentally appropriate activities to promote children’s learning and development such as tutoring services, computer training, sports programming, performance arts, math, and science programming. The reimbursable expenses may include instructor fees (other than program staff) and consumable products used for program delivery.Equipment - Funds may be used to purchase developmentally appropriate equipment for children between the ages of 5-15. A single equipment purchase reimbursement may not exceed $499.

Grantees must keep an inventory including depreciation schedule on all equipment purchased with Federal or State Funds.Staffing - Funds may be used to pay for school-age child care program staff salaries not to exceed 90% of the actual staff cost.Staff Development - Funds may be used to provide the required 12 hours of in-service professional development. At least 50% of these training hours must be obtained from an outside source. Professional development must be related to providing services for children ages 5-15 and their families.Travel Expenses claimed cannot exceed .38 per mile and no out-of-state travel will be reimbursed.Transportation - Funds may be used to provide transportation from the child’s school to the care site or from the care site to the child’s school. Funds allotted for transportation may be used for mileage, vehicle insurance, and the purchase of vehicles used for transporting children enrolled in the school-age child care program.

Expenses claimed for mileage reimbursement cannot exceed $0.49 per mile.Grant funds may not be used for the following:Direct service delivery including payment of tuition for children enrolled in the program.Care provided during the hours a child attends elementary or secondary school.Care provided on weekends and summer vacation.Care provided between the hours of 7:00 p.m. and 6:00 a.m., Monday through Friday.Care provided for children under the age of five (5).Transportation from the child’s school to the care site or from the care site to the child’s school.Drug testing, criminal history checks, first aid training, CPR training, or Tuberculosis tests.Goods and/or services that have received funding through other state, Federal, or philanthropic grant opportunities.Any other item deemed not eligible by the state. What is required of my program if we accept SACC funds? Grantees shall meet the following performance measures associated with the grant:Grantees shall submit claims for reimbursement within 60 calendar days following the end of the month in which a cost was incurred.Grantees must complete quarterly reporting on how funds are being utilized.Grantees will be responsible for developing and administering a family survey twice per program year. Grantees shall achieve a satisfactory or better rating on 85% of all completed family surveys at the middle and end of each program year.

If they do not, a Corrective Action Plan (CAP) will be issued by the State. Surveys should help to inform how grant funds are utilized in the school-aged program.Grantees are required to utilize 100% of their grant funding allocation. Failure to fully utilize the full allocation may result in inability to receive future grant funding from OECOSL. Do I have to pay back grant funds?

Because this grant is for cost reimbursement through the claims process, there is no requirement to pay back funds if your program closes or is no longer participating in the grant program. However, grantees who are found to have violated the terms of the application process or attestation, or who are found to have provided false or misleading information to OECOSL regarding eligibility requirements or spending, may be asked to repay funds, be deemed ineligible for future grant rounds, and/or be referred to Indiana’s Family and Social Services Administration Audits Department for additional action. How will I receive grant funds? Payments for reimbursement are submitted through a claims process for cost reimbursement.

Grantees shall submit claims for reimbursement within 60 calendar days following the end of the month in which a cost was incurred. Payments will be made within 30 days of the approved claim. Do multi-site organizations apply under one application, or do I complete an application for each site?

You will only need to complete one application for the organization. You will be asked to type in the legal name of your program/organization at the beginning of the application form. In the form under “Site Information”, you will be asked to list the name of your program’s site(s). If you have multiple sites for which you are applying for funding under this grant, you will be able to provide site information and funding requests for each site.

Please note that you can save and update your application and the corresponding number of sites at any point, up until you click “Submit” on the application page. How will applications be evaluated and scored? Step 1: Review for Mandatory Requirements and Complete Application (PASS/FAIL)In this step, proposals will be evaluated to ensure that they adhere to all grant requirements, including applicant eligibility. This section of the application will be evaluated based on the overall completeness and accuracy of organization information provided in the application.

Applicants must complete all questions regarding their organization information. Applicants that do not meet the eligibility and service requirements may be disqualified. In addition, incomplete proposals may be disqualified. Further, all applications received after the deadline set forth on the SBA Grant Opportunities page for the School Age Child Care Grant - SFY 2027 shall be disqualified.Step 2: Qualitative Review (Technical Proposal and Priority Criteria)Proposals will be scored based on the submitted Technical Proposal that is included in the application form.

This scoring will have a maximum possible score of 85 points. Evaluations may include one or more rounds of grant proposal clarifications or discussions (oral and/or written) between the State and the applicant focused on the details of the Technical Proposal, including the proposed use of funds.The following are the application scoring sections:Technical Proposal Scoring is worth 85 points.How will funds be used? If I receive the SACC grant, when will I receive payment? Claims can be submitted once the grant is finalized and executed, usually late in the calendar year, and can continue monthly through the end of the school year. You will be required to submit your claims and receipts within 60 days of the end of the month you are claiming for, and reimbursements are usually issued within 30 days of the approved claim. All claims will be due by no later than July 31 at the end of the grant term.

After I complete an application for my first site, how do I access applications for my other sites? You will only need to complete one application for the organization. After awards are finalized, grantees will receive a Claims Form from the FSSA Claims Dept. and a Budget Spreadsheet from the SACC Project Manager, with the breakdown of the award amount by site and in each of the four spending categories.

BOTH of these need to be submitted to FSSA Claims each month they are claiming. What else is expected from me if I am awarded the SACC grant? Grantees are required to participate in the Indiana Quality Program. Self-Assessment (IN-QPSA) at every program site within 90 days of their program’s start date.

This is a FREE online strengths-based self-assessment tool that enables programs to rate their performance based on the Indiana Afterschool Standards and Specialty Standards. For more information about the Indiana Afterschool Standards or to access the self-assessment tool, please click hereAdditionally, grantees shall administer a semi-annual family survey twice per program year and must receive a satisfactory or better rating on 85% of all completed surveys. How often do I need to survey my families? Grantees shall administer a survey at the middle and end of each program year.

Additionally, the program must achieve a satisfactory or better rating on 85% of all completed surveys. If they do not, a Corrective Action Plan (CAP) will be issued by the state. Do I have to provide receipts or documentation of how my program spent the grant funds? Yes, receipts will need to be submitted with each claim, and kept for taxes and audit purposes for three years, in order to show what the funds were used for specifically.

What is the deadline for my program to spend grant funds? The final claim submission will be due by July 31 of each grant year. Grantees are strongly encouraged to submit final claims as soon as the program has completed the school year to avoid any delays, especially if you will be out for the summer. Am I required to repay or return funds if my program closes after receiving the funding?

Programs submit monthly claims based on cost reimbursement for the before and after school program, only while the program is open and operating during the school year. If the program closes, they would not be required to return any funds. Copyright © 2026 State of Indiana - All rights reserved.
