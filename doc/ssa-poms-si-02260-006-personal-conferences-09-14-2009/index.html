BackgroundIn Page v. Schweiker, the Federal District Court for the Middle District of Pennsylvania held that an individual who requests a waiver of a Supplemental Security Income (SSI) overpayment is entitled to an opportunity for a personal conference with a decisionmaker when the waiver cannot be approved on the basis of the initial waiver request.Consistent with the decision in Page v. Schweiker, a SSI recipient must be provided an opportunity for a personal conference before a request for waiver of recovery can be denied.B.

PolicySSI recipients must be provided an opportunity for a personal conference before a request to waive the recovery of an overpayment can be denied. This does not change waiver processing when the waiver is allowed.Nor does this requirement change any waiver processing up to the point where you informally determine, based on current information (i.e., the file, the waiver request, and any documentation submitted) you cannot approve the request for waiver.1. Who Can Conduct a Pre-waiver Decision Personal Conference?Both service representatives (SRs) and claims representatives (CRs) (as well as higher level positions) can conduct the pre-waiver decision personal conference.The position authorized to conduct the personal conference (the decisionmaker) is the same as for making the overpayment disposition decision (the signature requirement). Refer to SI 02220.005C.4. for signature requirements.Unlike Title II, any position, any grade level, authorized to recommend or approve an overpayment disposition can conduct a pre-waiver decision personal conference.Authority to make a final decision on the disposition of an overpayment includes the authority to conduct the pre-waiver decision personal conference.The decisionmaker conducting the personal conference will usually be the decisionmaker that makes the waiver determination after the personal conference.2.

The Decisionmaker Must NOT Have Participated in Any Part of the Overpayment ProceedingsThe overpaid individual is entitled to a fair and full hearing before an impartial decision-maker. Therefore, a FO employee who has participated in the overpayment proceedings in a case cannot be a decision-maker for the pre-decision personal conference.Participation in the case means having made a determination about any fact that is material to the pending waiver determination: fault, ability to repay, credibility or veracity of the overpaid person.The decisionmaker participated in the overpayment proceedings when, after reviewing the request for waiver, the file (if any) and any supporting evidence, he or she made the preliminary determination to deny the request for waiver.The decisionmaker did not participate in the overpayment proceedings when he or she developed the underlying changes to the record that caused the overpayment.While an initial overpayment notice is a determination that an overpayment exists, mere preparation of this notice does not constitute participation in the overpayment if the author has not made any determinations about facts that are at issue in the waiver determination.EXAMPLE: As part of a scheduled redetermination, you verify the SSI individual's earned income. The verified income shows that the individual earned more than what was estimated. When the verified wages are input, the system computes an overpayment.However, you have not participated in the overpayment proceedings because you have not made a preliminary determination concerning any factor at issue in a request for waiver: without fault and whether recovery would defeat the purpose or be against equity and good conscience.Under these circumstances, you may conduct a personal conference.3.

Action When All Employees at the Appropriate Grade Levels Have Already Been Involved with the OverpaymentWhen all employees at the appropriate grade levels have been involved with the overpayment proceedings, arrange for a transfer of the case to a nearby FO. Advise the recipient of the need for the transfer.In rare cases it may be necessary to arrange for an authorized employee from another office to travel to the servicing FO to conduct the conference.Examples of “rare cases” include, but are not limited to, the distance between offices, the recipient’s or witness’ financial circumstances, etc.4. Personal ConferencesPart of the waiver decision process is to assess the credibility of the individual requesting the waiver. Offer the individual a face-to-face interview or choice of telephone contact or video conference in place of a face-to-face interview.5.

Folder ReviewPrior to the personal conference, the overpaid individual (and his or her payee/representative) has the right to review any information used to calculate the overpayment, and the applicable law and regulations. (Remember that the information used to calculate the overpayment might be stored electronically as well as in a claims folder.)The decisionmaker or another employee eligible to be a decisionmaker for the case must be prepared to answer questions.If the person asks for a telephone or video conference, you must mail a copy of the folder to him or her for review prior to the conference. Prior to mailing, remove material that either refers to another person or does not relate to the overpayment or waiver request.6. The Conference DecisionThe personal conference is an independent and thorough examination of all the evidence related to the overpayment and the requested waiver.

The decision requires a wholly independent attitude without any prejudice by the decisionmaker. The decisionmaker is bound by the requirements of the Social Security Act (the Act), the Regulations, and the policies and procedures in the POMS. The decisionmaker may not depart from nationally prescribed polices and procedures.7. Appeal of a Waiver Denial After a Personal ConferenceAfter a personal conference, the first level of appeal for a determination to deny, a request to waive a SSI overpayment is a reconsideration.

This is different from the denial of a Title II request for waiver where the first level of appeal is a hearing. This difference is the result of different regulations. The decisionmaker for the reconsideration of the waiver denial cannot be the decisionmaker that made the initial determination to deny the waiver. (See SI 04020.030B.2)8.

Individual Requesting Waiver Resides Outside the USThe agency will not hold a face-to-face personal conference outside the United States. To obtain a personal conference, the individual requesting waiver must return to the U.S., or choose to have the conference by telephone or video conference.C. Procedure1. Obtaining DocumentationWhen you are unable to obtain any documentation of the cause and/or the calculation of the overpayment, and you have nothing to refute the individual's claim that he or she is not at fault in causing the overpayment, find the individual not at fault.Do not determine the individual's ability to repay the overpayment.Waive the overpayment.2.

Scheduling the Personal Conferencea. Individual Resides in the USWhen a request for waiver of recovery of an overpayment cannot be approved in full, do NOT send a waiver denial notice (SSA-L8173-U3 or SSA-L8174-U4). Rather, prepare a notice of the proposed denial of waiver on a SSA-L8165-U2.Because you have not made an initial determination on the request for waiver, the notice scheduling the personal conference will not contain appeal rights. Send the initial notice of proposed denial of waiver in SI 02260.006D.1.Include dates for both the folder review and for the personal conference.The date of the scheduled conference must allow for at least 5 work days for the recipient to review his or her file.The recipient can waive the 5-day period for review by asking to review the information on the day of the personal conference.If the individual is unwilling or unable to come to the conference site (most often a SSA office), he or she may choose to have the personal conference conducted by telephone or videoconference.See SI 02260.006.C.3 for what to do if the person declines the opportunity for a folder review and/or personal conference or does not appear for the personal conference.See SI 02260.006.C.4 regarding the person’s options for how to conduct the personal conference and the FO’s responsibilities.b.

Individual Resides Outside the USFollow the same procedures as for a U.S. resident, but allow sufficient time for foreign mail delivery and for travel to the U.S.3. Person Declines a Folder Review or a Personal Conference or Does Not Appear for the Personal ConferenceWhen the offer of folder review is declined, the recipient still retains the right to the personal conference.If the person declines the folder review and the personal conference, make the waiver decision based upon all the evidence in the file.Input the waiver decision using the Direct SSR Update MSSICS (UODC, UOPD, and UOWV screens), see MSOM BUSSR 004.007, MSOM BUSSR 004.008, and MSOM BUSSR 004.009., prepare the notice (see SI 02260.006D.2) and document the file (see SI 02260.006C.5).When the recipient does not appear for the personal conference, contact the recipient by telephone to determine whether he or she wants to decline the personal conference.Reschedule the conference if requested.If unable to contact the recipient by telephone, reschedule the conference. Explain in the notice rescheduling the personal conference that failure to appear will result in SSA making the waiver determination based on the evidence we already have.If the person does not attend or declines the second scheduled conference, make the waiver decision based upon the evidence in the file. Input the waiver decision using the Direct SSR Update MSSICS (UOPD, UODC, and UOWV) screens.

See MSOM BUSSR 004.008 through MSOM BUSSR 004.009 and see SI 02260.006D.2.4. Folder Review and Personal Conferencea. Folder Review by Person (see GN 02270.009)Prior to the review, remove material that either relates to another person or does not relate to the overpayment or waiver request.Furnish copies of any requested material from the claims folder and/or relevant sections of the Act and Regulations.Do not charge the recipient for furnishing documents.If, after the folder review, the person decides he or she does not want a personal conference, see SI 02260.006C.3.When the scheduling letter is returned because it was undeliverable, develop a current address. (See SI 02220.051 for suggestions on developing a new address.) If a new address cannot be obtained, process the waiver as a denial based on the evidence you have.

Input the waiver denial using the Direct SSR Update MSSICS (UOPD, UODC, and UOWV) screens. (See MSOM BUSSR 004.007 through MSOM BUSSR 004.009).For additional information see GN 02270.009.b. Selecting the Conference SiteProvide suitable private space, generally in the FO. If the individual cannot come to the conference site (e.g., he or she is incapacitated or in prison), the FO can conduct the personal conference by telephone or videoconference.Consider conducting personal conferences at locations other than the FO on a case-by-case basis, and only in those limited circumstances where a claimant has exhausted all other means of obtaining a personal conference, or where conducting a personal conference by any other means would be so inadequate owing to a claimant’s physical or mental condition as to infringe upon the person’s right to a full and fair hearing on the request for waiver.If there is a legitimate reason why the person cannot use any of these alternative methods for a conference, the decisionmaker must travel as far as necessary to conduct the conference.

You cannot make the initial waiver decision until the person has had reasonable opportunity for a conference.When there is a legitimate reason that the recipient cannot come to the conference site or participate in a telephone or videoconference, and SSA cannot go to the person, or for other reasons the personal conference cannot be held, approve the request for waiver. (UOWV screen, see MSOM BUSSR 004.008 through MSOM BUSSR 004.009).When the individual refuses without a legitimate reason to come to the conference site or to have a telephone or videoconference, he or she forfeits the opportunity for the conference.Make the waiver decision based on all the available evidence (i.e., the material in the file).See MSOM BUSSR 004.008 through MSOM BUSSR 004.009 for various screens used to input the decision.c. Conducting the ConferenceAlso see GN 02270.013.Prior to starting the conference, explain that:The decisionmaker has not been previously involved in the case.The decision will be based on the evidence presented as well as the evidence in the file.The purpose of the conference is to provide the individual with the opportunity to present his or her case and to furnish evidence to support it.Identify all participants and explain their roles.Determine whether the individual has reviewed the file.If he or she has not and now wants to do so, provide the opportunity.Document that the individual has not but now wants to review the file. See SI 02260.006B.5.Provide the applicable law and regulations.Orally provide a summary of the evidence already in file.Invite the individual to present his or her case.WitnessesAdvise the individual that SSA cannot compel the presence or testimony of a witness at the personal conference.

Use your best efforts to obtain the presence of the witness(es) or to obtain his or her answers to written questions. When your efforts are unsuccessful and the recipient continues to demand the presence of the witness(es), escalate the case to a hearing.Inform the individual if additional evidence must be obtained before a decision can be made.When the recipient insists on a personal conference and, despite all efforts the conference cannot be held, approve the request for waiver.5. Notice and Distribution of the Personal Conference DecisionPrepare, date, and sign the special determination (SSA-553) documenting your personal conference decision. Include sufficient information on the SSA-553 to document the file that the recipient was given the opportunity to review his or her folder and whether he or she attended the personal conference.

Include whether the individual reviewed his or her file and whether he or she contested any of the information reviewed.The SSA-553 documents the explanation of decision you enter under the caption “Based on the facts you gave us, we have decided:” on the Notice of Waiver Denial (Form SSA-8173-U3 or Form SSA-8174-U4). (For instructions on completing the SSA-553, see GN 01010.370.)Further action by the decisionmaker will depend on the type of the waiver decision.NOTE: Nothing precludes the decisionmaker from preparing and issuing the notice of waiver decision.a. Full WaiverWhen the decision is to grant full waiver of recovery of the overpayment, follow instructions in “SSI Overpayment – Basic Requirements Concerning Waiver” (SI 02260.001) and enter the waiver approval using the Direct SSR Update MSSICS (UOWV) screen (see MSOM BUSSR 004.007 through MSOM BUSSR 004.008) with the appropriate reason code (i.e., REASON #5 – APPROVED – EXCESS RESOURCES WITHIN TOLERANCE).b. Denial or Partial WaiverWhen the decision is to deny in full the request for waiver or to grant waiver of recovery for part of the overpayment, enter the denial or partial approval using Direct SSR Update (UOWV) screen.

See MSOM BUSSR 004.007 through MSOM BUSSR 004.008. The decisionmaker prepares and transmits the notice on a SSA-L8173-U3 or a SSA-8174-U4, as appropriate.Use the language in SI 02260.006D.3.c. DocumentationUse the UOWV screen to document the DECISION AMOUNT and REASON for the approval or denial.D. PROCEDURE-NOTICES1.

Initial Notice for Proposed Denial of WaiverUse the SSA-L8165-U2. Do not include the reconsideration paragraphs. The notice must include the following.The reason the waiver cannot be approved (including the basis for findings as to fault and defeat the purpose, or against equity and good conscience).The appropriate statutory and regulatory basis with a reference to either:section 1631(b)(1) waiver of recovery andsection 1621(e) responsibility of an alien's sponsor of the Social Security Act.Always cite sections 416.550-416.555 of title 20 of the Code of Federal Regulations.The date for the individual to have a personal conference and the date to have the opportunity to review the folder. Allow 5 days for folder review.Do not address recovery of the overpayment (i.e., neither request refund nor propose adjustment).2.

When the Person Declines Folder Review and Personal ConferenceWhen the person declines folder review and/or personal conference or does not appear for the personal conference and declines use of alternative methods (i.e., telephone or video conference), send a SSA-L8173-U3 (for a current payment case) or a SSA-8174-U4 (for a non-payment status case). Use the following language under the caption “Based on the facts you gave us, we have decided:”(Fill-in a. or b.) Therefore, we are denying (fill-in c.) request for waiver. (Insert your rationale for denying the request for waiver from the SSA-553.)(You/She/He) advised us that (you/she/he) did not want a personal conference on the proposed denial of (your/her/his) waiver request.(You/She/He) did not attend the personal conference for (you/her/him) on (date and time) to discuss the proposed denial of (your/her/his) waiver request, nor did (you/she/he) ask us to reschedule it or conduct it by telephone or video conference.(your/her/his) 3. Denial or Partial Waiver (including when the person does not appear for the personal conference)Prepare and send an SSA-8173-U3 (for a current pay case) or an SSA-8174-U4 (for a non-payment status case) following the personal conference if you fully or partially deny the request for waiver.

Include the following language under the caption “Based on the facts you gave us, we have decided:”(Fill-in a. or b.) (Insert your rationale for denying the waiver or granting only partial waiver from the SSA-553.)(You/She/He)(were/was) notified that we had scheduled a personal conference for (you/her/him) on (date and time) to discuss the proposed denial of (your/her/his) waiver request, but (you/she/he) did not come in for the conference nor ask us to reschedule it or conduct it by telephone or video conference.As a result of c. personal conference, we have determined that d. or e.(your/her/his) our initial “Notice for Proposed Denial of Waiver” to deny your/her/his request for waiver of recovery of the overpayment was correct.(you/she/he) will not have to repay the total $______ which (you/she/he) incorrectly received for _____ but repayment of $________ is required.E.
