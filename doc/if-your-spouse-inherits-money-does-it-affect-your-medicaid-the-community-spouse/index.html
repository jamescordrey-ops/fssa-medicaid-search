If your spouse inherits money, does it affect your Medicaid? The community spouse rules

Reference notes Federal

Program: Federal

Source: Find on FSSA site ↗

The plain answer first: if you are on Medicaid (a waiver, or nursing home care) and your spouse inherits money from their own family, in your spouse's own name, after your Medicaid eligibility is established, that inheritance does not take your Medicaid away. It is your spouse's money, not yours. Why, in two rules. First, the snapshot: the couple's assets are counted once, when eligibility is determined.

A snapshot of the couple's assets is taken to determine the community spouse's share (the Community Spouse Resource Allowance), and the snapshot reflects the couple's assets at the time of the Medicaid applicant's first continuous institutionalization. The spousal share is set from that snapshot; money your spouse receives later is not re-counted against you. Second, the name on the check: the community spouse is allowed to keep all income that is solely in his or her name, plus half of all jointly owned income. An inheritance left to your spouse alone is solely in your spouse's name.

Who the money is left to is what decides it. The Medicaid spouse is allowed only $2,000 in non-exempt assets to stay eligible. If the inheritance were left to you, the person on Medicaid, it would count as your income in the month received and as your resource after that, and a large inheritance would put you over the $2,000 limit until it is spent down or otherwise handled under the rules. An inheritance left to your spouse does not do that.

Two cautions. Keep your spouse's inheritance in your spouse's own name and account: money held jointly can be treated as partly available to the Medicaid spouse. And if your spouse later applies for Medicaid themselves, the inheritance counts for their own application at that time. These spousal protections apply to nursing home care and to in-home and community care under the Medicaid waiver.

If a large inheritance is coming, tell your case manager before the money moves, so the accounts and the paperwork stay clean.
