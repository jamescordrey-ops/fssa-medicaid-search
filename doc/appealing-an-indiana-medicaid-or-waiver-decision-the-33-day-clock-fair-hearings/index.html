Appealing an Indiana Medicaid or waiver decision: the 33-day clock, fair hearings, and the PAR route

Reference notes Indiana

Program: General

Source: Find on FSSA site ↗

If you get a letter saying your Medicaid or waiver benefits are changing, being cut, or denied, and you do not agree, you can appeal. An appeal is a request for a hearing before an administrative law judge (ALJ) with the Office of Administrative Law Proceedings (OALP). The hearing decides whether the decision was correct. The clock is 33 days.

For a waiver service authorization, your appeal must be received by the OALP within 33 calendar days of the service authorization. For waiver decisions generally, the appeal is due within 33 days of the date of the notice. For a benefits change notice, the deadline is 33 days from the issue date printed on the formal notification. For a Division of Family Resources (DFR) eligibility action, you appeal by sending a written request to your local DFR office or to the Hearings and Appeals Section of DFR within 33 days of the date of the action or issue being appealed.

One exception to know: an appeal of an objective-based allocation (OBA) is due within 30 calendar days, not 33. How to appeal: the request must be made in writing and forwarded to the hearing authority. The notice you received must describe the decision that was made, your appeal rights, how to appeal by requesting a Fair Hearing, and the deadline. Your case manager may inform you how to prepare the written request, help you prepare it, and tell you the time frames and the address for submission.

Because of conflict-free case management rules, the case manager may not file the appeal or appear at the hearing for you unless the case manager is your Medicaid authorized representative on your Division of Family Resources record. If your letter is about your waiver budget or allocation on the Community Integration and Habilitation (CIH) Waiver, there is a step before an appeal: a Personal Allocation Review (PAR). Your individual support team may request a PAR through your case manager, using a Long-Term Budget Request (LTBR) that states the reason, for example that the Algo level is incorrect, the ICAP assessment has significant errors, or the ICAP addenda (behavioral and health factors) are incorrect. The Bureau of Disabilities Services (BDS) reviews the LTBR within seven business days of submission.

If more documentation is needed, the waiver specialist may ask your case manager for it; if it is not provided within 90 days, the request to modify the allocation is denied. If you are dissatisfied with the waiver specialist's determination, you may appeal the service authorization within 33 days of the date of the notice, and during that appeal the BDS maintains the budget from the last agreed-upon budget allocation. Note: the Family Supports (FS) Waiver has a capped allocation that is not subject to appeal. If you do not understand the letter, do not wait to act.

The 33-day clock runs from the date on the notice whether or not the letter is clear. Ask your case manager to explain the decision and help you prepare the written request, and get the appeal in writing before the deadline. For Medicaid eligibility decisions, appeals are governed by IHCPPM chapter 4205 (section 4205.00.00, Appeal request; section 4205.26.00 covers continued benefits while an appeal is pending).
