Skip to main content Close Menu HIP Work Requirements Partner Toolkit Beginning Jan. 1, 2027, certain Healthy Indiana Plan members ages 19–64 will need to complete 80 hours each month through work, part time education, job training, or community service—unless exempt. To help raise awareness of these changes, FSSA has created a partner toolkit. The toolkit will be updated with additional information leading up to the January 1 start date.

What counts as an exemption from HIP Work Requirements? How to prepare now for HIP Work Requirements? FAQ What are the HIP Work Requirements? You can meet the rule by working/volunteering/training for 80 hours, earning enough money, or being in school at least half time.

When do Work Requirements begin? Requirements begin for new applicants and redeterminations initiated on or after January 1, 2027. For applicants in January, this means that requirements must be fulfilled for October, November, and December. Who must comply with HIP Work Requirements?

Most HIP members ages 19–64 must meet work requirements unless they qualify for an exemption. What exemptions exist? People who are pregnant, caring for kids, have disabilities or serious health needs, or drug or alcohol treatment programs, or meet other criteria don’t need to complete hours. What counts as medically frail?

FSSA is still working with the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services on the medical frailty requirements for HIP Work Requirements exclusions, including the stipulation that the condition significantly impairs the individual’s ability to comply with work/community engagement requirements. More guidance will be made available. Could members of my household have different redetermination dates? Yes, it is possible that members in the same household could have different redetermination dates.

You can find your redetermination date in the Benefits Portal, under Assistance Group. Where can I find my redetermination date? You can find your redetermination date in the Benefits Portal, under Assistance Group. Is it possible my redetermination date could change?

Yes, and you will be notified if it changes. You can also find your redetermination date in the Benefits Portal, under Assistance Group. Can you tell me when my three month lookback period will begin? For new applicants to be approved for HIP, the lookback period will be three months before the application month.

Example: Apply in January, look back will be December, November and October. For existing HIP members, the lookback period will be three months before the renewal month or three months before the quarterly work requirements review. Example: Renewal month is May, look back will be April, March and February I received a Notice of HIP Work Requirements and was curious about the 80 hour requirement…does income over $580/month mean I don’t also need the 80 hours? Correct.

The requirement is either 80 hours of qualifying activity or income equal to federal minimum wage multiplied by 80 ($7.25 x 80 = $580). I am considering enrolling in an online college, would the classes have to be part of a degree program, and can they be online? Coursework at an accredited institution of higher learning, as well as high school or GED courses, can be counted. Classes may take place in-person or online.

I am self employed with a LLC partnership, so I do not have a paycheck, and the split income annually with my spouse. We both work 40–50 hours weekly. How should we maneuver this? An individual can provide documentation of the hours of self-employment and/or earnings by the most recent year’s tax returns or other business documentation.

I currently live off modest returns from personal investments. If I show $580/month or more, does that count as self employment without tracking hours? All income that is counted in Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) budgeting counts towards meeting work requirements. This would include any income which is taxable, as well as Social Security income.

What counts toward the 80 hours? Any mix of work, volunteering, classes, or job training counts. How is compliance checked? The state checks every three months.

If they can't confirm your hours or exemption, they’ll send you a notice asking for proof. What happens if someone does not comply? Coverage stops, and you must reapply. You also can’t get Marketplace financial help during this time.

How often are renewals? You renew HIP every six months, and the state checks your hours every three months. How do members report hours or changes? Report changes in work, school, health, or family within 10 days using the online portal, phone, mail, or a DFR office: Benefits Portal: Submit information to the Division of Family Resources via your FSSA Benefits Portal account at FSSABenefits.IN.gov Mail: FSSA Document Center, PO Box 1810, Marion, IN 46952 Phone: Call DFR at 1-800-403-0864 In-Person: Visit your local Office of the Division of Family Resources during regular business hours - 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., Monday – Friday, except for State Holidays What is the Benefits Portal?

The Benefits Portal is the easiest way to report hours and manage your HIP coverage. What should members do now? Make sure your info is correct, check if you have an exemption, plan how you’ll meet the rules, and set up an Benefits Portal account.
